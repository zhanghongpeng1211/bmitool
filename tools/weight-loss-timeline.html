How It Works

This calculator projects your weight loss timeline using established metabolic equations. Here is the math behind the projection:

Step 1: Calculate Your BMR

We use the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which research shows is more accurate than the older Harris-Benedict formula for modern populations:

Men: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) + 5
Women: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) − 161

Step 2: Apply Activity Multiplier

Your BMR is multiplied by an activity factor to get your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE):

  • Sedentary (1.2): Desk job, little to no exercise
  • Lightly Active (1.375): Light exercise 1–3 days/week
  • Moderately Active (1.55): Moderate exercise 3–5 days/week
  • Very Active (1.725): Hard exercise 6–7 days/week
  • Extremely Active (1.9): Physical job + hard training

Step 3: Model the Deficit

A pound of fat contains approximately 3,500 calories. To lose 1 lb per week, you need a daily deficit of 500 calories. The timeline is calculated as:

Weeks to Goal = (Total lbs to lose × 3,500) ÷ (Daily deficit × 7)

Metabolic Adaptation

As you lose weight, your BMR drops slightly because there is less body mass to maintain. The calculator applies a 5% metabolic adaptation factor to keep projections realistic. Very aggressive deficits (750+ cal/day) may trigger stronger adaptation and are harder to sustain.

Practical Tips

  • The "Moderate" scenario (500 cal/day deficit) is sustainable for most people and yields ~1 lb/week loss.
  • Aggressive deficits work short-term but increase muscle loss and rebound risk.
  • Save multiple scenarios to compare approaches. All data stays in your browser.
  • Plateaus are normal. If your weight stalls for 2+ weeks, recalculate with your new weight and adjust the deficit.
